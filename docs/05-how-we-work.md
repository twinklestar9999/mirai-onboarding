# How we work

By the end of this page you'll know what a normal day looks like here, and what "good"
looks like when nobody can see you working.

**Time:** ~15 minutes to read
**You'll need:** chat access

---

## We are remote and mostly async

The team is spread across timezones. That has one big consequence: **the person who
can answer your question is often asleep.**

So the default is *async* — you write something down, and it gets answered in a few
hours rather than a few seconds. That feels slow at first. It's a trade: you get long
uninterrupted stretches to actually think, and in exchange you plan around not getting
instant answers.

Working with that instead of against it:

- **Ask early in your day**, so an answer can arrive before you finish. A question
  asked at the end of your day costs you a whole day.
- **Ask in public channels, not DMs.** A DM reaches one person, who may be off. A
  channel reaches whoever is awake, and the answer stays searchable for the next person
  with your question. DMs are for things that are personal, not things that are trivial.
- **Never block on one answer.** When you post a question, pick up something else while
  you wait. Always have a second thing you could be doing.
- **Over-share progress.** Nobody can see your screen. If you don't write it down, from
  the outside it's indistinguishable from nothing happening. This is not surveillance —
  it's how you get help before you need rescuing.

What matters more than exact hours:

- **Be predictable.** Roughly the same hours each day so people know when to expect you.
- **Say when you're away.** Stepping out, appointment, sick day, finishing early — a
  one-line message in the channel. Nobody needs a reason.
- **Don't work late to look committed.** It's invisible, it doesn't help, and it burns
  you out on a timeline that always ends badly. Stop at the end of your day even with
  something unfinished — write down where you got to and pick it up tomorrow.

## Standup

<!-- TODO(team): is standup written or a call? What time, which channel/link, what
     format? -->

Whatever the format, write three things:

1. **What I finished** — done, not "worked on"
2. **What I'm doing today** — one or two things, specific
3. **What's blocking me** — the most important line, and the one juniors always skip

"Blocked" doesn't only mean stopped. It means anything slowing you down: waiting on a
review, waiting on access, stuck on a bug for hours, not sure what the ticket means.
Say it. Standup exists so that blockers surface in a day instead of a week.

A good junior standup looks like:

> Yesterday: finished the validation on the signup form, PR is up.
> Today: picking up the error-message ticket.
> Blocked: my PR needs a review before I can build on it — could someone take a look?

## Your 1:1

You'll have a recurring 1:1 with your manager. <!-- TODO(team): cadence -->

This is **your** meeting, not a status report — they can already see your tickets. Bring:

- What's going well and what isn't
- Anything you're confused about, including things you feel you should already know
- What you want to be learning
- Feedback, both directions

If you're struggling, say it here first. Managers can fix almost anything they know
about and nothing they don't.

## Writing well is the job

On a remote team, most of your visible output is writing: tickets, PR descriptions,
questions, standups. Clear writing gets you help faster and makes your work count.

The trick is boring: **say the thing first**, then the context. Not "I was looking at
the auth module and noticed... and then I tried... so anyway is the token expiry
wrong?" but "Is the token expiry meant to be 15 minutes? Here's what I found."
